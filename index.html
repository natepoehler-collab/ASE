What this is

An asset-selection engine — it ranks tradeable instruments across crypto, US stocks, forex, indices, commodities, metals & oil by how much tradeable movement each made over the last year on 1-minute data.

How a "move" is counted

This is the heart of it. We walk each asset's 1-minute price over the last year (every crypto, stock, metal, oil and index that trades as a perpetual future comes from its perp on Bitget, KuCoin, OKX or MEXC; forex, and anything with no perp, from Binance, massive.com or Dukascopy) and mark out its moves. The key idea: a move only ends when price turns around enough to say "that run is over." A move ends when price pulls back from its high (on an up move) or its low (on a down move) by two things at the same time:

  • by at least the pullback filter you picked (explained right below), and
  • by at least that class's minimum — 5% crypto & stocks, 3% commodities / metals / oil, 1% indices & forex — so a tiny wiggle can never end a real move.

When both happen, the move is finished and its end is pinned at the highest point it reached (up move) or lowest point (down move) — then we start measuring the next one. A swing is counted as a tradeable move if it's at least the move size you picked. Stretches where nothing that size is happening are simply "not in a move" — we never invent moves that aren't there.

The two knobs — pullback filter & move size

The two rows of buttons above the table decide how the whole table counts moves (they work on every asset class):

  • Pullback filter — 25% / 50% / 75% / 90%. This is how much of a pullback you're willing to sit through before you call a move over — how much heat you'll take while you hold. It's the one that takes a second to click, so here it is plainly:
    • 25% = twitchy. The moment price gives back a quarter of the run, the move's done. You get lots of smaller moves — you bail on every dip.
    • 90% = patient. You hold until price has given back nearly the whole run before calling it over. You get few, big moves — only the major trends, and you sit through deep pullbacks to keep them in one piece.
    • 50% and 75% are in between. It's the exact same price data every time — you're only choosing what counts as a move worth caring about. Lower = noisier, more moves. Higher = calmer, only the big trends.
  • Move size. The smallest move you want counted — 5%, 6%, … 20% for crypto, and each class has its own ladder (forex starts at 1%, metals at 3%). Pick 5% and every 5%+ run counts; pick 20% and only the 20%+ ones do. Picking a size re-ranks the table by that size and reframes the size-based columns (tot%, in / not in, avg gap, participation) around it.

So: pick a pullback filter and a move size, and the whole table — every asset — re-counts itself that way. It's a direct dial for how much chop you're willing to trade through to ride a trend.

The size columns

The ≥5%, ≥6%, … ≥20% columns are cumulative counts at the pullback filter you've selected: a 20% move is also a 5%+, 6%+, … move, so one big move shows in every column up to its size. The smallest column already is the total move count; the bigger columns just tell you how many got large.

The Oct-10-2025 crash

(Crypto.) That day's impossible wicks are repaired in the data. The crash itself is now counted as a normal move — it ends when it pulls back like any other — exactly as it appears on the chart.

What each column means

Several columns below are built around the move size you've selected (the highlighted button), counted at the pullback filter you've selected — so they all shift when you change either knob. That size is shown in those column headers, e.g. "tot% in 5%+".

  • now — the row's current position (changes when you sort).
  • re-sort rank — each asset's fixed rank by how many selected-size moves it made; glued to the asset so you can see where it lands when you re-sort.
  • tot% — every selected-size move's size added together: the total % of price action to capture.
  • avg gap — average time between tradeable selected-size moves.
  • in / not in — the % of the time price was / wasn't actively in a selected-size move (since we're always in the trade).
  • the ≥X% columns — count of moves of at least each size, at the current pullback filter. Cumulative — a big move is in every column up to its size.
  • its moving — green ▲ / red ▼ + size + how long it's been in that move (days / hours): at the last refresh, whether price is right now in a move of at least the selected move size, which way, and for how long. This one IS price direction.
  • price — the asset's latest price as of the last refresh, with its daily % change right beside it (green up / red down). The % uses each market's own convention: crypto = the rolling 24-hour change (the figure exchanges like Binance show); US stocks = change vs the prior session's 4:00pm-ET close; forex / indices / commodities / metals / oil = change vs the prior day's close (one that trades as a perp, around the clock, has no daily close, so it shows the rolling 24-hour change).
  • "short history" tag (small text under an asset's symbol) — flags an asset with less than a full year of 1-minute data, usually a recent listing (e.g. the XAUT gold token, or a freshly-IPO'd stock). Its move counts cover a shorter window than everything else, so read them in that light. Only the few assets that lack a full year are tagged.
  • 7d range — the highest and lowest price over the rolling last 7 days, and beneath them the % between them (low → high). A small % means a quiet, chopped-up week; a big % means real movement — a fast chop-vs-trend tell. Two colors here share one reference: the weekly open (the price 7 days ago). The % color = whether the middle of that 7-day range sits above (green) or below (red) the weekly open. The wo + ▲/▼ = whether the current price is above (green ▲) or below (red ▼) that same weekly open. So the % color tells you where the week's range centered; wo ("weekly open") tells you where price sits right now — both measured against where the week opened.
  • mkt cap rank · 30d — the coin's current global market-cap rank (#1 = biggest), with how many spots it has climbed (green ▲) or fallen (red ▼) over the rolling last 30 days shown beneath it. Up the rankings = gaining size/relevance vs the rest of the market; down = losing it. Crypto only ("new" = it wasn't ranked 30 days ago).
  • 30d move participation — the same measure as in / not in, but over just the last 30 days: the % of that window spent inside a ≥ selected-size move (since we're always in the trade). Green if it's in a move right now, red if not. High = it's been consistently moving lately; low = mostly quiet.
  • Vol — a speedometer of how current trading activity compares to its own rolling-daily average. Needle straight up = right at average; it sweeps left and turns red below average, right and turns green above. The ends are ±100%: full right = volume running 2× its average or more (marked DH), full left = essentially no volume (marked DL) — the DH/DL tags show only when it's pinned at that extreme. "Current" is the most recent 5 minutes for crypto and the most recent hour for stocks / forex (the latest update). This is buying & selling ACTIVITY, not price direction — a green needle means more trading is happening, not that price is going up. It only reads while the market is open (crypto is 24/7; stocks include pre- & post-market). Volume for crypto & stocks, tick activity for forex, metals, oil & indices; soft commodities are too sparse to gauge, so no needle there.

How to read it

  • Click any column header to sort; click again to reverse.
  • Top of the selected-size column = the assets with the most tradeable movement.
  • Set the pullback filter from twitchy (25%) to patient (90%) and the move size to how big a move you care about — the whole table, every class, recounts itself.

The other three tabs

The class buttons — crypto, US stocks, forex, indices, commodities, metals, oil — all show the ranking table above. EMA, Lifer and Trendy are different tools with their own rules, and all three are crypto only.

EMA — cross setups

Two EMA pairs, 9/16 and 12/21, watched across nine timeframes: 5m, 15m, 30m, 1h, 2h, 3h, 4h, 8h and D. The 9/16 is the fast trigger and the 12/21 is the confirmation, so the order they cross in is the whole signal:

  • A 9/16 cross opens a setup on that asset only while the 12/21 still disagrees — that's pending. A 12/21 cross on its own never opens one.
  • It turns valid when the 12/21 then crosses the same way. If both cross on the same bar it is born valid.
  • Any flip back against the open direction clears the status — pending or valid. Out of pending it simply clears; out of valid, that same 9/16 flip re-opens the asset on the same bar as pending the other way.
  • A valid row lives 8× its own timeframe, then clears: 5m→40m, 15m→2h, 30m→4h, 1h→8h, 2h→16h, 3h→24h, 4h→32h, 8h→64h, D→8 days.

Every duration is measured from the bar's close — the moment the signal was first knowable — not from when the app happened to notice. Every crypto asset has a row on each of the nine timeframe triggers: the trigger chooses which timeframe's status, direction, valid price and flip time the row reads from, and an asset with no live cross there shows blank status cells rather than leaving the table. The tenth box, new, is a different view — valid rows only, the newest flip on each of 15m, 30m, 1h, 2h, 3h and 4h. Every timeframe is computed on every pass, so switching is instant.

Lifer — where price sits against the 21/200 band

Lifer is a state, not a signal list: every crypto asset is always on it, though the screener hides the rows that do not match whatever you have selected. The EMA21 and EMA200 form a band on each of 1m, 3m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 1h, 2h, 4h and 1d, and each timeframe reads:

  • Long — the close is strictly above both. Short — strictly below both. Inbetween — between them, which is not a valid state and ends the run.
  • A run is stamped at the close of the candle that started it, and entered at that close — so "pnl since cross" is measured from the crossing candle's close, not from the EMA level it cleared.
  • Retest — price coming back to the band and being rejected keeps the run alive; a close through the band is an acceptance and ends it. Rejections are confirmed on 1-minute candles for the 3m–30m rows and 5-minute candles for 1h and slower, so a rejection shows as it happens rather than at the row's own next close. The 1m row has no finer candle to use, so there a rejection lands on its own close like everything else. An acceptance is always judged on the row's own close. The cell reports the last band test, and an acceptance outlives the run it ended — so a row that currently reads long or short while showing accepted is showing the close-through that ended its previous run, not a break of the run it is in now.
  • 200 cross / retest — when price last crossed the EMA200, and whether it has since retested and rejected off it.
  • Stoch RSI — %K with %D and which way it is moving. A completely flat RSI window shows nothing rather than 100, because 0/0 read as maximally overbought on a straight collapse and on any frozen feed.
  • Volume "n/20" — the mean volume of the last 20 closed candles against the mean of the 100 before them, plus how many of those 20 individually beat that baseline. So 14/20 means the recent run is broadly heavier, not that one spike dragged the average.
  • Spike — a different question from the volume "n/20" column, and the one that catches a sudden burst: the newest closed candle's own volume against the mean of the 20 candles before it. Nothing is averaged with it, so a one- or two-candle burst reads as itself — a candle carrying ten times the recent normal reads 10x, where the 20/100 volume chip reads that same candle as about 1.45x because it is one of twenty in the numerator. It is volume only: the candle does not have to be large, or green, or anything else. On the second candle of a sustained burst the multiple falls, because the first one has entered the baseline — heavy volume that keeps up becomes the new normal, which is the honest reading. The thresholds are 1.5x, 2x, 3x, 4x, 5x and 10x. On the Lifer tab the multiple is recomputed from the live 1-minute stream the moment each candle closes, not carried from the last engine pass. It has its own column, beside volume, sortable like any other. The cell turns green when it meets whatever threshold you have selected in the spike drop-down — with none selected, nothing is coloured. A blank cell means the multiple is not known for that bar, which is not the same as 1x: the live window needs 21 unbroken candles, and leaving the tab closes the stream, so it blanks until the buffer refills.
  • pnl 1h / 4h — the market move over the last 1h and 4h of that asset's own bars, signed by the row's side: on a short row a fall in price shows as a green gain, the same convention as the pnl column. A row sitting inbetween has no side, so the cell is blank. The screener's moved filter is a different test — it selects on the size of that move over 4h, 12h or 1d regardless of direction, and only requires the direction to agree when you have also set the side chip.

The pattern column is price structure, not candlestick shapes: a triangle, a channel or a flag fitted to the swing pivots, replayed over the last 300 candles of that timeframe. The fast timeframes (1m, 3m, 5m, 15m) are recomputed on every pass; 30m and slower are carried for up to an hour, since a 4h structure cannot change in a minute.

Lifer alerts — parameter sets

Save the screener and timeframe you are using as a named set, then arm it from the alert drop-down (🔔 marks an armed set). Armed sets are checked as new data arrives and ping you when an asset newly matches.

  • Each alert latches per set and per asset: you get one ping when an asset starts matching, not one per refresh. It re-arms for that asset once the asset stops matching.
  • Only a judgement made on current data can move that latch, so switching tabs or looking at a stale payload can neither fire a ping nor silence a real one.
  • Several sets can be armed at once, and each bell toggles only its own set. Sets are independent, so two armed sets that both match the same asset will each ping you.
  • Arming re-announces whatever already qualifies, then goes quiet until something changes. That is deliberate: arming a set is how you ask "what matches right now?", so it tells you instead of waiting for the next transition.
  • A ping needs live data. The asset's newest bar on the set's timeframe must be the bar that just closed. With another tab in front, the fast timeframes often never get there — so each armed set prints what it has actually observed, including ⚠ fires ONLY while this tab is open. Read that line before trusting a 1m or 3m set.
  • You can change an alert after you save it. Pick it in the set drop-down, adjust any condition or the timeframe, then press update. The alert keeps its name, its id and its armed state — it is the same alert with new parameters, not a replacement. While what is on screen differs from what is saved, the button highlights and the line beside it reads edited and shows the conditions still stored. save as new makes a second alert instead. Changing the conditions clears that alert’s memory of what it has already told you about, so it re-announces whatever now qualifies — the same thing arming does.
  • The timeframe is part of the set. Pick 1m, 3m, 5m or any other row first, then save — the set's own timeframe is shown beside its name in the set drop-down, and picking the set switches the table back to it. So a 1m spike alert and a 4h band alert are two sets, armed independently, each judged on its own timeframe.
  • The Lifer 1m feed decides how FAST an alert reaches you (the feeds switches beside the tab buttons). With it on, a closed candle is judged within about a second. With it off, the row falls back to the engine's published state — which lands about a minute or more after the candle closed (the engine's minute run; measured 72–93 s on a copy of your data while the old app shared the computer) — so a 1m alert still fires, just about a candle late — and on 1m it is judged inside a 3–5 second window before the next candle opens, so a slow pass can cost that bar its check (it re-fires on the next one if the row still matches). Do not take that from this paragraph: each armed set prints what it has actually observed, and that line is the authority.
  • A set needs at least one condition — saving with nothing selected is refused.
  • If more assets hit at once than one notification can hold, you get one notification that still names them, and the in-app log keeps the complete list — no asset is reduced to a count you cannot recover.

Trendy — how one-sided the recent candles are

For each of 5m, 10m, 15m and 30m, over your chosen window of 20, 40, 60, 80 or 100 candles:

  • % green — the share of candles that closed up, out of the green plus red only. A doji is neither, so it is left out of the denominator entirely rather than counted against green.
  • Run — how many candles in a row, right now, are going the same way.
  • Move (headed pnl) — the first candle's open to the last candle's close, as a percentage.

Alerts tab — build, arm and review every alert

One place for every alert in the app. An alert is a name plus any combination of conditions from any tab, and it fires when all of them hold on the same asset at the same moment.

  • Build one — type a name, then pick each condition from the drop-down. + add condition adds another; ✕ removes one. Each condition is something a tab already shows, read the same way that tab reads it:
    • Stoch — Stoch RSI %K on any timeframe at / above or at / below a level, or crossing up or down through it; %K above or below %D, or crossing it (the Lifer stoch arrow); the Stoch Jackpot presets (all 7 timeframes agree, or 3m–4h agree and the 1m turns toward them).
    • Volume — the volume ratio (optionally on 12 of the last 20), the volume spike, and the table's Vol gauge.
    • Lifer — side; when the run started (the cross time); retest rejected or accepted; retested the 200 (Y); crossed the 200; band gap; distance from the 200; pnl; pnl 1h / 4h; pattern and breakout; moved X% over 4h / 12h / 1d (in the side condition's direction when the alert has one, as on the Lifer screener); or any saved Lifer screener set.
    • EMA — the 9/16 cross state (pending / valid, long / short), the 9/16 and 12/21 directions, and the % move since the valid cross, on any EMA timeframe.
  • Under the form, Fires when spells the alert out in words, and matching right now shows how many assets it would match on the data already on screen.
  • Create saves it; arm starts it alerting. Edit loads it back into the form and save changes updates it in place — same alert, still armed if it was. Delete needs a second click on confirm delete within 5 seconds.
  • An alert only fires on current data. A stoch reading must be advanced by the live stream on a bar that is the one just before the bar forming now; a Lifer or volume condition needs that timeframe's newest bar to be the one that just closed; move, pattern, Vol gauge and EMA conditions exist only in the engine's publish and need one less than 3 minutes old. Anything stale can neither send a ping nor reset the alert.
  • One ping per asset when it starts matching, then quiet until it stops matching on current data. An alert with a crossing condition also never pings the same asset twice in one minute, so a stoch wobbling across a level cannot flood you. If more than 8 assets match at once you get one notification that names them all.
  • Stocks, metals, oil and indices that trade as perps have the stoch, volume and Lifer-state conditions but no move, pattern, EMA or screener-set data, so an alert that uses those only matches crypto.
  • The status column says whether an armed alert can actually fire right now, and if not, why (the Lifer 1m feed is off, the stream is not connected, the EMA data is stale).
  • Delivery — every alert is a Windows notification plus a flashing taskbar icon, and optionally a webhook (Discord, or an SMS service) you paste in. Send test fires a test alert. History keeps the last 100 alerts sent, with the values that fired them.
  • Lifer screener sets saved on the Lifer tab are listed here too and can be armed, disarmed and deleted here; their conditions are edited on the Lifer tab. Stoch Jackpot alerts made in an earlier version were carried over as combined alerts.
  • Armed alerts work on every tab, which keeps the 1-minute stream open while any alert is armed. Disarm them all, or turn the Lifer 1m feed off, and it closes.

Live feeds — the per-tab off switches

Beside the tab buttons, feeds holds one checkbox per live stream. Both are on unless you turn them off, and the choice is remembered across restarts. Unchecking one closes that socket immediately — it is not throttled or filtered, it is not opened at all. The figures below are what each stream costs while its own tab is up all day; each one already closes when you leave its tab, so unchecking it saves that bill for the hours you would otherwise have spent on that tab.

  • live price (~480-640 MB/day on the Crypto / EMA tabs) — the table tabs' live price and day%, streamed from each asset's perpetual-futures contract. Off, those two tabs show the price the engine last published, which moves when the engine publishes.
  • Lifer 1m (~480-640 MB/day for the crypto board; ~850 MB/day or more while the Stoch Jackpot tab is open or an alert is armed, which adds the perp-traded stocks, metals, oil and indices) — the 1-minute closed-bar stream the Lifer tab overlays onto the engine's state. The price column and this stream share the same perp connections, so with both on you pay for one set, not two. Each full reconnect re-downloads about 8-9 MB of recent candles. Off, Lifer rows only move when the engine publishes, and the spike multiple is no longer recomputed per candle — so a 1m signal reaches you roughly a minute or more late instead of within a second. It does not stop alerts firing.

Those figures come from a 60-second capture on 2026-09-17 (Bitget 24.4, OKX 24.8, MEXC 32.6 bytes a second per symbol; the KuCoin part was not captured and is assumed similar), scaled to the full list — a short window, so treat them as a rough guide, not a ceiling.

These two switches are not the whole picture, so here is every lever, plainly:

  • The two feed switches (above) — the live streams the Lifer / crypto / EMA tabs use. Per-tab, instant, remembered.
  • The three source toggles in Data refresh setup below — these stop the engine downloading from a source, which is the only way to cut the stocks, forex, indices, commodities, metals and oil traffic. Turning one off freezes those instruments on the data they already have; nothing is deleted.
  • The crypto 1-minute download is covered by the crypto source toggle, but bear in mind every tab's numbers are computed from it — turning it off freezes the whole crypto board, not one tab.
  • The pop-out chart window opens its own 1-minute stream for the one symbol it shows, for as long as it is open. Neither feed switch covers it; closing the window ends it.

Data refresh setup

Refresh pulls fresh 1-minute data and re-runs the scan automatically (and on launch): everything that trades as a perpetual future every minute, three seconds past the minute (the perp venues are keyless), and forex and the instruments with no perp every hour (those sources are rate-limited / slower). The three toggles below are on by default; uncheck one to stop that source updating and keep its current data.

Crypto comes from its perpetual-futures contract (Bitget, KuCoin, OKX or MEXC), or Binance / KuCoin spot for a coin with no perp — no account, no API key. On by default — uncheck to stop updating this source and keep its current data.

Forex, and the indices, commodities and metals with no perp, come from Dukascopy; the metals, oil and indices that trade as perps come from their perp every minute. No account, no API key. This toggle covers both. On by default — uncheck to stop updating this source and keep its current data.

US stocks that trade as perps come from their perp every minute (no key needed); the rest come from massive.com, which needs your own free API key:

  1. Go to massive.com and click Sign Up — it's free, no credit card.
  2. Open your account dashboard and copy your API key.
  3. Paste it below and hit Save. (Free tier is ~5 calls/min, so the stock refresh runs slowly.)

On by default — uncheck to stop stock updates. (massive's ~15-minute delay makes no difference at an hourly cadence.)
